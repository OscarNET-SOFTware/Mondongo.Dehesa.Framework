## Overview
This framework was created with the aim of providing an organized, modular, and reusable set of .NET libraries for developing modern applications, covering both *frontend* and *backend* domains, as well as shared modules and cross-cutting utilities. The ecosystem is designed to facilitate project structure, promote best practices, and add a touch of identity and originality through its naming convention.

The chosen name for this framework and its libraries is not accidental: it is a personal tribute to my roots and family ties with [Extremadura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extremadura). Although I was not born there, my parents are originally from this land, and for as long as I can remember, Extremadura —*especially [Solana de los Barros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solana_de_los_Barros), in the [Tierra de Barros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tierra_de_Barros) region*— has been my usual holiday destination, a place for family reunions, and a connection to the culture, nature, and traditions of Extremadura.

For this reason, this framework is also intended as a small tribute to that land, which is part of my story and to which I have a special affection.

## About the use of "Mangurrino", "Bellotero" and the naming convention in this project
In this project, I have chosen to use the terms "Mangurrino" [(*listen to pronunciation*)](./assets/audios/spanish-pronunciation-of-mangurrino.mp3) and "Bellotero" [(*listen to pronunciation*)](./assets/audios/spanish-pronunciation-of-bellotero.mp3) as part of the naming convention for the .NET libraries, thus giving the solution a unique, local, and fun identity.

> **NOTICE**
>
> *Both terms are used here **without any pejorative or insulting intent**. On the contrary, they aim to pay tribute to the cultural and linguistic richness of Extremadura, bringing a touch of humor and local pride to the project.*
> *Next, I would like to clarify the origin and meaning of these terms, as well as the reason for their application in the code architecture.*

### Why "Mangurrino" and "Bellotero"?
* "Mangurrino" is a colloquial term traditionally associated with the inhabitants of the province of [Cáceres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_C%C3%A1ceres). Its origin is popular and, although it can have different meanings depending on the context, it is usually used affectionately to refer to someone who is simple, down-to-earth, clever, or somewhat mischievous. In Extremaduran culture, "mangurrino" is never used as an insult, but rather as a loving or traditional nickname.

* "Bellotero" is used to refer to the inhabitants of the province of [Badajoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Badajoz), alluding to the acorn, the emblematic fruit of the Extremaduran [dehesa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dehesa) [(*listen to pronunciation*)](./assets/audios/spanish-pronunciation-of-dehesa.mp3) and a symbol of the region. Calling someone from Badajoz a "bellotero" is a friendly and popular way to highlight their origin and connection to the land.

### The analogy of the acorn and the geography of Extremadura
In a playful way, in some areas of Extremadura, the "mangurrino" is the name given to the upper part of the acorn (the cap or "hat" that covers it), while the lower part, the fruit itself, is associated with the acorn proper. This analogy is transferred to the geography of Extremadura:

* Cáceres is located in the northern part of the region, like the top of the acorn (cap or "mangurria"), hence the term "Mangurrino".

* Badajoz is in the south, like the bottom of the acorn (fruit or acorn), hence the term "Bellotero".

Thus, the naming convention not only reflects popular identity but also the geography and wit of Extremadura.

### Why are they applied to *frontend* and *backend*?
* I have decided to associate `Mondongo.Mangurrino` with libraries oriented towards the *frontend* (user interface, presentation, visual components, etc.), evoking the visible and approachable character of the top of the acorn and the north of Extremadura (Cáceres).

* On the other hand, `Mondongo.Bellotero` identifies the *backend* libraries (business logic, data access, services, etc.), symbolizing the robustness, depth, and rootedness of the bottom of the acorn and the south of Extremadura (Badajoz).

This convention makes it easy to quickly identify the purpose of each module and reinforces the Extremaduran identity throughout the architecture.

### Why "Mondongo" and the names of the framework and shared modules?
* `Mondongo` [(*listen to pronunciation*)](./assets/audios/spanish-pronunciation-of-mondongo.mp3) is a nod to Extremaduran gastronomy, especially to the tradition of the [matanza (slaughter)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pig_slaughter) and to [morcilla de mondongo (a typical blood sausage)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_sausage), products deeply rooted in local culture. The term is used here as a common and playful link for theentire library ecosystem.

* For shared, common, or cross-cutting modules (utilities, contracts, models, helpers, etc.), the term `Mondongo.Iberico` has been chosen, referring to the integrative character and the Iberian/Extremaduran heritage of the solution.

* The global name of the framework, `Mondongo.Dehesa.Framework`, pays homage to the most representative natural ecosystem of Extremadura: the dehesa. In this way, it reinforces the idea of a set of robust, diverse, and deeply rooted tools in the local identity.

> **IN SUMMARY**
>
> *This naming convention not only organizes and clarifies the code architecture, but also celebrates the diversity, humor, and pride of being from Extremadura, making this project something unique and memorable.*
